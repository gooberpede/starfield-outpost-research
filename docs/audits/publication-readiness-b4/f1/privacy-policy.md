# B4-F1 policy and scoped privacy decisions

The invoked F1 brief settles first-party saves/screenshots as eligible research
evidence, informed by community practice. **This is a maintainer policy decision,
not an express Bethesda/ZeniMax licence or a claim that legal risk is eliminated.**
No external legal research, rights-holder contact, new dependency or outside code
was used. Existing Bethesda content notices and third-party restrictions remain.
The old B4 R1 generic absent-written-permission hold is superseded for first-party
eligibility/local preparation; exact membership and publication approval remain
pending. R2 accepts character names and `C:\Users\User` historical provenance;
public instructions use repository-relative paths. R3 contributor wording and
R4 final excerpt extent remain B6/maintainer decisions. No legal text is changed.

HIST-0255 is first-party by maintainer-accepted provenance from the original
authorized local Saves inventory. Hash join against protected scan-04 recovers
Save41 as its original basename. Save52=HIST-0267 is a different identity. The
local identity registry documents lookup source, all exact original filenames,
available copies and missing old original locations; filenames were not obtained
by editing/re-decoding saves. Every selected original basename is resolved.

Prior B4 privacy receipts are reused only for matching SHA-256 identities.
Thirty-two selected saves already had full decoded ASCII/UTF16 indicator screens;
seven selected files needed targeted extension: F055–059, F064 and F067. F1
verified every BCPS stream EOF/length and scanned all decoded bytes in memory
for email, ASCII Steam-ID shape, Windows user paths, PEM and common GitHub token
shapes in byte/UTF16LE views. Sixteen email-shaped matches were examined in their
binary contexts: three repeated short numeric/binary patterns in each L save,
one repeated numeric/binary pattern in H36; none is an identified email account.
H21 produced no matches. No other tested indicator matched those seven files.
The exact offsets, domains, decoded lengths/hashes and context remain local in
`targeted-privacy.json`. Reader hash and source hashes are recorded there.

All thirteen selected screenshots match previously reviewed hashes. F1 re-read
the five existing contact sheets covering all twenty B4 images and the original
L3 caption/CS/LO1 source ledgers. No incidental chat/account/profile/notification
or desktop overlay was observed. B4's JPEG segment receipts report JFIF APP0 only,
no EXIF/XMP/COM. Resized visual inspection can miss fine details; this is a
practical privacy screen. Originals were not edited or re-encoded. Restriction
means Area is restricted; white preview does not prove completed construction.
Screenshots do not establish a same-time save/reference/XYZ or UI-cache join.

These are scoped preparation decisions, not exhaustive forensic clearance.
Opaque binary account identifiers, embedded preview imagery, all unrelated
quest/mod/platform fields and historical effective overrides were not exhaustively
decoded. No concrete sensitive identifier was established in the selected screens;
these stated residual limits are not converted into generic legal holds. B6 must
review their acceptability for exact distribution. No save sanitization is allowed.
The third-party donor F061 remains excluded without a separate permission decision.
Other local evidence stays private for the specific scientific reasons in the ledger.
