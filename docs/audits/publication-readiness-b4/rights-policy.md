## File inspection and practical privacy

[File dispositions](file-dispositions.csv) bind 71 actual byte identities to safe
local registry IDs. Full filenames/paths, raw header fields, pattern offsets and
private hit context stay in ignored `.local-work/b4/`. Character names are
acceptable under the brief; none is rejected for that reason. Candidate save
filenames may encode character text; names are preserved privately rather than
inventing sanitized archive names. No save contents are redacted or rewritten.

36 first-party saves underwent physical BCPS closure, all-stream zlib EOF/length
and checksum inspection, header parsing and full logical-byte ASCII/UTF-16
indicator screening in memory. Six additional L-series controls had exact hash
and first-stream/header checks only; their privacy result is explicitly PARTIAL.
Eight additional unique E3/S1/S2/SD1/D1 originals were read/hash/stat-checked on REWORK only; privacy remains NOT REVIEWED and their disposition is LOCAL-ONLY. The donor was hash-identified without opening its gameplay contents for privacy
review. No save was loaded. Header build strings are observed metadata, not an
originating executable hash or proof of effective overrides. Timestamp candidates
and filename dates do not establish a linear playthrough history.

The full-save screen tested Windows user paths, email shapes, a specific ASCII
Steam-ID shape, PEM private keys, common GitHub/AWS token shapes and quoted
password/API-key/secret assignments. There were 89 email-shaped byte matches
across 8 distinct match hashes. Local context inspection finds short sequences
inside binary data, with repeated non-text numeric patterns; they are not
verified email disclosures. No other listed detector matched those save streams.
The exact offsets/context are retained privately, never pasted into this report.
No false-positive match is labelled an account identity. This is a scoped screen:
opaque binary identifiers, nonstandard PII, embedded preview imagery and every
unrelated quest/mod/platform field were not exhaustively decoded. No claim of
an extraordinary save-publication prohibition follows from those limits.

All 20 JPEGs were hash-paired with existing CS/L3/LO1 ledgers and visually
reviewed using five local contact sheets. The views show game scenes/scanner,
map and panel UI; no chat, account/profile, desktop notification or personal
path overlay was observed. Resized contact-sheet review can miss very small
details; this is practical inspection, not a forensic guarantee. A direct JPEG
segment walk found only 14-byte JFIF APP0 metadata, no EXIF/XMP/COM or other APP
payloads. Windows image APIs exposed only image quantization properties, not
author/GPS metadata. Metadata extraction and contact sheets are local review
derivatives only; the original images were not altered. No screenshot establishes
a same-time save/reference/XYZ or UI-cache epoch join. L3 captions retain that
the beacon rejection says Area is restricted, and white preview is not proof
of completed construction or permanent scenery removal.

The first supplement lookup stopped because an old original path was missing.
Hash-identical protected copies were available; the corrected lookup records
missing original locations explicitly. The initial donor-root guess was wrong;
the source study identified the actual third-party root, where the exact manifest
hash was found. Neither diagnostic is used as proof of corruption or absence.

## Git-history assessment and limits

The refreshed scan inspected all 2,981 blobs / 58,774,886 bytes reachable from
the local refs returned by `rev-list --objects --all` across 100 commits; no blob
was skipped. Current starting-tree bytes were scanned separately with the same
detectors. **196 historical blobs** had Windows user-path indicators, versus
RR1's older 182/2,751-blob census. Those are blobs, not distinct users or 196
leaks. No enumerated blob matched the other detectors; none contained NUL bytes.
No identified credential is silently removed. Exact refs, paths, object IDs and
indicator offsets remain in ignored `history-scan.json`/`history-summary.json`.

This reuses RR1's stated pattern classes, adding one specific Steam-ID shape;
it is not a commercial secret scanner, entropy analysis, PII/schema decoder or
rights classifier. Unusual tokens, encodings and proprietary text can escape.
Dangling objects, reflogs, remote-only refs, remote assets/account settings and
future refs are outside coverage. `--all` includes local app checkpoint refs;
it is not a decision to publish them. No remote query or history modification
occurred. R2 and exact proposed public history remain B6/maintainer decisions.
