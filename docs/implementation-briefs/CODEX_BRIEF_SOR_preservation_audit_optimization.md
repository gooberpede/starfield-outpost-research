# Codex Implementation Brief — SOR Preservation Verification Audit and Material Optimization

**Authority:** Maintainer-approved offline audit, implementation, disposable qualification, and independent review. **No authority** to relax project-wide protections, modify original evidence or saves, launch/attach to Starfield, stage/commit/push, publish, or deploy revised verification rules to other investigations.

**Background:** Recent offline research (D5C/D5D/D5E/D5G) repeatedly reported preservation inventories of 77,893–93,334 pins. D5G reported 93,334 checks before and after work, with an eight-worker after-audit, but no trustworthy complete elapsed-time breakdown in the authored review. The inventory may include extensive historical receipts, derived data, saves and other archives unrelated to current scientific inputs. A 0.1% reduction is not meaningful. The project must preserve science, saves and historical evidence without repeating disproportionate full-corpus verification on every ordinary read-only research task.

**Read first and honor:** `AGENTS.md`, `WORKFLOW.md`, `docs/RESEARCH_EXECUTION_PROTOCOL.md`, `docs/EVIDENCE_STANDARD.md`, `docs/SAVE_PROTECTION.md`, `docs/EDITORIAL_STANDARD.md`, `docs/templates/completion-contract.md`, `docs/reviewers/research-completion-reviewer.md`, permissions, publication and provenance policies. Relevant existing receipts, methods and review records: `docs/experiments/resource-predictability-contrast/review.md`, `docs/experiments/resource-construction-demand/review.md` and their ignored `.local-work/` audit artifacts. The existing rules continue to govern all work until the maintainer expressly approves any replacement.

## Governing question and non-negotiable outcomes

Determine precisely what the broad preservation audits verify, why the pin inventory grows, what time/I/O they consume, and whether routine redundant **historical-evidence** verification can be reduced by **at least 80%** for ordinary offline investigations without materially weakening preservation. Implement and qualify a replacement mechanism in isolation; provide a concrete maintainer-ready policy proposal and deployment plan. **Do not silently install, activate or interpret that proposal as current policy.**

An 80% reduction is a mandatory design/qualification threshold, **not** permission to skip inputs essential to science or protection. Freeze baseline, representative workloads, accounting domains and measurement method *before* evaluating replacement performance. Report failure honestly. If safe attainment is not possible under permitted approaches, return REWORK or narrowly justified ESCALATE—not a smaller “successful” target, a cosmetic 0.1% optimization, or an altered denominator.

## Scope and execution sequence

### Stage 1 — Reconstruct and freeze the baseline

1. Reconstruct the D5G 93,334-entry before/after audit from existing scripts, manifests, receipts and logs. Do **not** rerun the full historical audit merely to obtain timing. Identify exact timestamps where available and separate measured durations from estimates and missing values. If receipts lack valid timing fields, explicitly say so; any fresh representative timings use *disposable or read-only* controlled fixtures and must be identified as such.
2. Produce mutually understandable breakdowns by directory, evidence category, protection class, file/record type, and *task-relevance*: critical originals; task-used scientific inputs; historical immutable evidence; intermediate/derivative; transient/ignored diagnostics; other/unknown. Account for overlapping categories and physical files vs archive members vs logical pins. Explicitly reconcile historical counts D5C 77,893, D5D/E 78,435, D5G 93,334 without assuming each increment denotes new unique evidence.
3. Record entries enumerated, unique files read, total bytes read, actual cryptographic hash invocations, bytes cryptographically hashed, wall time, CPU (if reliably measurable), peak memory and scratch storage (if available), cache effects/parallelism, and number of repeated operations. Distinguish pre/post audit from relevant scientific-input verification, save checks, review rechecking and archive validation. Unknown quantities remain UNKNOWN; no fabricated timing or extrapolated I/O represented as measurement.
4. Define two frozen representative workflows: **W1** a D5G-like ordinary offline read-only analysis and **W2** a higher-risk game/save-handling protection-control simulation, performed only on disposable stand-ins. Record coverage/integrity requirements and equivalent work units. Do not operate the game, touch an original save, or run a live experiment.
5. Establish a baseline-protection ledger of *what must remain detectable* for each class and operation, including edits, deletions, substitutions, inserted unexpected paths, wrong baseline identities, changed EXE/ESM, modified protected original, and maliciously altered audit manifest. Establish what cannot be guaranteed absent an external immutable root of trust. Baseline authoring alone is not validation.

### Stage 2 — Design, implement and qualify a substantive replacement

1. Compare **at least two materially different** viable designs (for example task-scoped verified-input manifests plus Git/protected-path change detection, versus immutable evidence archive/Merkle or signed manifest validation with targeted rehashing). Assess integrity guarantees, threat model, stale metadata risk, workspace trust, cost, migration complexity and failure modes. Do not claim ordinary mtimes/sizes reliably detect content substitution. Determine which safeguards are achievable by trusted Git object identity, protected ACL/read-only controls, immutable out-of-tree baselines, filesystem snapshots or other justified methods; state actual trust assumptions.
2. Implement the preferred replacement **as an isolated prototype** and draft exact changes to the durable project rules/scripts needed to adopt it. Prefer small reusable tools and compact output rather than massive new receipts; no deletion, compression, rewriting, normalization or repacking of historical evidence. Existing broad audits stay available as a fallback. Keep output under ignored local workspaces unless approved authored documentation belongs in the repo.
3. **Pass/fail optimization gate:** For W1, eliminate **at least 80% of routine redundant historical-evidence verification work** under the frozen historical category and comparable protections. Compute reductions against unchanged baseline across *enumeration operations, unique files re-read, cryptographic bytes read/hashed, and wall-clock cost*. No cherry-picking: show every denominator and each component separately, plus W1 total audit cost and end-to-end verification cost. A >=80% entry-count saving alone with comparable bytes/time is **not** sufficient. The proposal must deliver a material reduction in repeated historical reads/hashing and demonstrate credible measured time benefit (with environment noise/variability disclosed). If one metric legitimately cannot reach 80%, treat that as a mandatory exception for maintainer decision, not a silent pass. Do not count operations merely moved to hidden setup work or performed by another process as eliminated; amortization must be explicit.
4. For W2, do **not** require an 80% saving at the expense of protection. Preserve the existing high-risk safeguards (including protected-save before/after verification, installed binary/definition identities and free-space guards as applicable). Measure performance and risk separately; it is acceptable for W2 to show little or no reduction.
5. **100% of actual task scientific inputs** must retain explicit hash-backed identity verification at intake and where applicable upon reuse/change. Critical original saves/backups, installed game inputs and native evidence are never weakened merely to satisfy the 80% target. Protected originals may never serve as disposable fixtures.
6. On a disposable copy/fixture, independently inject alteration, same-size content substitution, deletion, renamed/replaced artifacts, unexpected addition, manifest tampering, stale/incorrect baseline and available timestamp-preserving mutations; also test benign authorized new task outputs and normal Git changes. Define expected detection/allow outcome before running. Include tests of failures in the scanner itself (I/O error, permission denial, truncated archive, corrupt hash receipt, interrupted scan). Demonstrate detection, explicit fail-closed handling and non-destructive recovery. Include a negative control and independently execute representative tests rather than merely inspecting code. Do not assert protections against adversaries who can alter both data and its trusted root unless such protections are really implemented.
7. Ensure no new preservation mechanism silently treats a previous audit's “PASS” as proof of the present scientific-input bytes or permits modified untracked/ignored raw evidence to escape required checks. Specify when a full historical re-audit is triggered (e.g. archive migration, suspected corruption, incident, explicit release gate, changed trust root), without turning that trigger into a permanent blanket tax for routine offline work.
8. Provide a concrete migration/rollback plan and a maintainer-facing policy diff, clearly flagged **PROPOSED / NOT EFFECTIVE**. The maintainer must separately approve activation and any amendments to `WORKFLOW.md`, `docs/RESEARCH_EXECUTION_PROTOCOL.md`, `docs/SAVE_PROTECTION.md` or other controlling policies. Do not implement task-wide relaxation by editing those rules during this assignment.
