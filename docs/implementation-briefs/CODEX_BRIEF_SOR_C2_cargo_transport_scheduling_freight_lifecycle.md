# Codex research brief — C2: Cargo transport, freight lifecycle, scheduling and remote operation

**Project:** `starfield-outpost-research`  
**Type:** Bounded but thorough offline native/definition/script/save investigation, independent review  
**Phase:** C2, following accepted C1 configuration/UI research and S1/S2 local storage/logistics research  
**Authority:** Offline research, read-only analysis and authored documentation or narrowly necessary evidence-checking utilities only. **No game launch, process attach, debugger/observer/plugin implementation or deployment, save creation/modification, protected-source modification, Git staging/commit/push, or publication.** Mentioning an experiment does not authorize it.

## 1. Goal and decisive questions

Explain **the complete cargo-ship freight cycle** for ordinary same-system and inter-system outpost links: when and why a ship loads, departs, arrives, unloads, returns, waits, resumes or fails; which inventories actually change; what happens to excess or undeliverable items; and how transport progresses when the player and endpoints are remote or unloaded. Investigate **the scheduling machinery and every consequential modifier** especially thoroughly. Derive a useful causal model, not merely a list of scripts, function names or animations.

The motivating observed anomaly is the user's report of an outgoing cargo container filling and resources subsequently appearing in the *incoming* compartment at its origin. Test the specific **return-of-undelivered-freight hypothesis** against the actual transport/load/unload paths. Preserve it as a hypothesis until supported; distinguish return freight, deliberately bidirectional trade, local output-link redistribution and unrelated incoming inventory writers.

Use the real Save52 Feynman I (“Li Cu xF4”) ↔ Feynman VII-b (“Nd Al He3”) **ordinary same-system link** already identified by C1 as the principal grounding case, including its paired pad markers, child containers and common cargo ship. Do **not** mistake empty saved outbox/inbox/ship inventories for evidence that the route has never operated, or assume the saved snapshot is synchronized with earlier screenshots. Where available, choose one independently grounded inter-system example for comparison; otherwise substantiate its mechanism from actual definitions/native contracts and report the save-evidence boundary.

**Mandatory answer-first deliverable:** A concise table for each major mechanism stating **what actually happens**, necessary conditions, exact timing/quantity/modifier inputs, saved versus transient state, evidence and consequential unknown. Follow it with a full example journey trace, including a partially blocked destination and return leg. Plain English first, technical provenance next; do not mask decisions behind phrases such as “conditionally mapped.”

## 3. Required investigation

### A. Authoritative ship/pad state machine and complete journey

Identify actual pad/ship roles, identities, reference ownership/pair relationships, transport registers, current route and direction, ship assignment/creation/retirement, active versus idle states, and whether a route can involve multiple ships. Trace **all materially relevant stages**: eligible load, quantity selection, ship inventory mutation, dispatch/departure, simulated travel, arrival, unload, return-leg loading, departure back, origin unload, next-cycle rearming. Distinguish scripted intent from engine-admitted action and asynchronous completion. Produce an evidence-linked **state-transition table** (event/condition → source state → destination state → side effects → next state) and a normal round-trip trace. Clarify whether freight naturally flows both ways via the two outgoing compartments and exactly which incoming compartment receives each leg.

### B. Scheduling, timing and modifiers — priority investigation

Trace **the actual timing authority** from scheduler/tick/event producer to state change, including whether timing is per-ship, pad, route, Workshop, cell, task queue, Papyrus timer, physics/animation marker or shared elapsed machinery. Identify timer initialization, persistence, reset, pause/rearm, acceleration/catch-up, and dependence on player presence, local versus UT/game time, frame time, wait/sleep, fast travel, global TimeScale, and any per-planet or distance conversion. Do not substitute E1’s production clock for cargo’s without a proven call/dataflow connection.

**Inventory of modifiers is mandatory:** derive all positively discovered contributors to departure readiness, minimum load, ship capacity, loading/unloading rate or quantity, flight duration, distance/system category, fuel availability, transport state, script properties, actor values, perks, game settings and difficulty/other overrides, where applicable. For each identify **where defined, read, multiplied/clamped/compared, and whether persisted or live-derived**. Distinguish *identified absence within a qualified path* from unsearched possibilities. Look for config/definition constants, script waits/timers, actual call arguments, update intervals and queued tasks. If an aspect is animation-only, prove the separation rather than assume it.

Provide formulas/pseudocode where native evidence supports them, with units and floating-point/rounding semantics where consequential. Work at least **two contrasting timing examples** with explicitly labelled grounded operands versus hypothetical values; do not invent exact ship durations, rates or numbers. State if ships depart empty, partially full, or only at a threshold, and what starts the next departure. Identify clocks and state machine interactions capable of changing throughput, including how long-running cycles behave under large elapsed-time jumps.

### C. Inventory flow, saturation, and returned cargo

Trace actual **source decrement, ship increment, ship decrement and destination increment** functions; item selection and order; allowed forms, capacity (including effective ship capacity), mass accounting and partial fit; when inventories are read/re-read; atomicity, staged operations, and failure/rollback/retention behaviour. Explicitly distinguish transfer from the independent S2 local redistribution and direct production writers. Explore both **one-direction and reciprocal** freight, with partially filled ships, mixed item stacks, partial destination capacity, zero capacity and full receiving bays.

For the user's origin-incoming anomaly, derive falsifiable native predictions: Can an undelivered item remain aboard and be carried back? If so, where does it go at the original pad, and through which **incoming/outgoing** compartment? What alternative writers could place items in the origin incoming compartment? Are unoffloaded items retained, returned, deleted, retried, redirected or left aboard? Identify route reversal and hold/cargo ownership precisely. A clear negative answer is valuable if the mapped operation rules it out under ordinary conditions. Do not infer cargo returns from historical mixed inventory alone.

### D. Remote/unloaded transport and interruptions

Trace whether cargo movement progresses without visible ship 3D, loaded cells, nearby player or currently resolvable pad/ship refs. Distinguish **registered and resolvable**, graphically unloaded, handle-unresolvable, Workshop-unregistered, route-disconnected and fuel-gated states. Link concrete global schedulers and event/task callbacks to shipping updates; trace effects of waiting, sleeping, fast travel and ordinary elapsed gameplay. Establish whether missed transitions accumulate, replay, skip, reconstruct or simply stall; determine what is saved about in-flight cargo, timers, route stage, target and pending work, and what is rebuilt on reload.

Include route removal, destination retargeting, pad deconstruction, changing availability, disabled references, ship loss/despawn and fuel interruption **within the ordinary state machine**: where cargo ends up and what resumes operation. Do not claim every bug edge is handled; explain ordinary recovery and actual identified unhandled conditions. Treat failure of reference resolution as a state to explain, not proof of a gameplay bug.

### E. Save/runtime reconstruction and corroboration

Provide an **entity/field correspondence matrix** for both endpoint pads, child cargo containers, the ship and any transport controller: definitions → saved records/fields → loading/remapping → runtime state → dispatch and inventory effects. Mark PERSISTED, DEFINITION-DERIVED, RECONSTRUCTED, TRANSIENT or UNKNOWN. Reuse verified C1 Save52 identities and source pins; check any additional historical saves for **positive** transport-stage or stocked-ship examples if accessible at reasonable cost. Historical inventory is a snapshot, not a movement trace. When a save field is missing or undecoded, investigate the **specific necessary representation** sufficiently to answer the C2 question or state a concrete narrow boundary, not propose a general save extractor.

A meaningful conceptual cycle derived from code is acceptable without newly recorded live gameplay. Distinguish an independently replayable calculation from a mere plausible narrative, and an actual historical load/event from a load-reachable native path.

## 5. Evidence threshold and stopping discipline

Use the project's agreed **reasonable engineering confidence / explanatory sufficiency** approach. Accept `STRONG` native causal findings with qualified save support as working knowledge where the mechanism explains the behaviour and no concrete contrary path matters. `PROVEN` requires decisive scope-matched evidence. An unobserved live trace does not automatically preclude accepting a coherent, native-derived ordinary transport model. Conversely, simply identifying a schedule variable or script callback does not satisfy **scheduling and modifiers** research. Unsupported hypothetical exceptions are not equally weighted alternative explanations. If a required state/clock or return-cargo distinction remains genuinely undecidable offline, record the **specific missing operand/path**, what was checked, competing predictions and the minimum discriminating test; do not mask a missing mandatory investigation as completion.

Investigate promising in-scope native and script paths, including indirect calls/virtuals where materially implicated, without expanding into arbitrary game-wide shipping, full BCPS writer development, debugger backend, or generic simulation implementation. Do not insist on proving all imaginable failures. No live observer should be recommended without showing it would distinguish the actual remaining hypotheses and that its information gain justifies engineering risk.
