## Q2 — Bursts, losses and writer availability

QPC frequency is 10,000,000 ticks/s. First admitted event is QPC 2619875036404;
last is 230.9878599 seconds later. Thirteen admission-order timestamp inversions
confirm that sequence is reservation/admission order, not engine scheduler order.
Charts use QPC bins; the synthetic arrival replay sorts recorded timestamps.
Fourteen recorded thread IDs and per-kind/thread/epoch strata are retained locally.

| Phase | Actual evidence and uncertainty |
|---|---|
| Startup/main menu | One READY record. Main-menu request/ACK collection bounds are 53.066–57.442 s after first record. No exact menu transition time is inferred. |
| Load/pre-travel | Lifecycle activity at 125–128 s; pre-travel request/ACK bounds 159.600–165.159 s authenticate 110 records and zero losses. Operator reports Save52 loaded without travel. |
| Travel-period activity | Remaining 19,670 records occur at 221.099–230.988 s, between pre-travel and arrival checks. Most admitted activity is in two dense groups; the 223-second bin contains 5,445 records. Causation by travel itself is not measured. |
| Arrival/STOP | Arrival request/ACK bounds 245.054–249.957 s; STOP collection at 250.143 s. No additional admitted records after 230.988 s. Silence is not proof of native inactivity. |

The lossless framing and loss-free admission meanings differ. Of 29,737 counted
admission outcomes (19,780 successes + 9,957 full-queue failures), approximately
33.48% fail admission. This is not the fraction of native calls lost: one path
emits several records, an inactive emit is not necessarily counted, and dropped
types/source IDs/timestamps were not captured. Provider entry/return plus
selected/visited account for 19,452 admitted records; only 24 protected decisions
and nineteen direct samples occur. High-volume bypass witnesses are scientifically
relevant to prior activity; their volume alone does not justify removal.

The [writer data](d5y/writer-progress.csv) shows increments of 5,931 drops across
ACK rows 6–9; 439 across 12–14; 55 at 18; 2,215 across 20–21; and 1,317 at 30.
Other reported counter intervals add zero. This proves uneven counter increments,
not exact burst durations or absence of drops between every native event. Each
counter is sampled separately. ACKs contain no QPC timestamp; the endpoint
event's QPC is **not** the flush/ACK time.

Queue high-water lower bound reaches 1,024. Enqueue/dequeue sampled differences
also reach 1,024, corroborating pressure, but are not atomic occupancy readings.
Terminal producer retries are 89 and retry-exhaustion losses zero. All losses
are reported capacity losses; output faults, overflow and worker failure are
absent. Terminal enqueued/dequeued/written/flushed/acknowledged all equal 19,780.
The writer makes 366 write calls and 78 flush calls, including ACK operations.
Maximum WriteFile call time is 1.1267 ms; maximum combined raw/ACK flush time is
1,206.5674 ms. The earlier maximum 287.5799 ms already appears at ACK 2,
before reported loss. The larger maximum first appears at ACK 20. Metrics are
formed after raw flush and before that ACK's own append/flush, so the new maximum
cannot be assigned specifically to ACK 20's raw flush; ACK 19's flush can contribute.

The actual worker drains a finite enqueue snapshot, pops batches of at most 64,
writes raw, flushes raw, appends and flushes its cumulative ACK. It checks guards,
disk, requests/STOP and sleeps 20 ms. A reserved but unpublished head can stop a
drain even when later cells are ready; no reservation-publication latency is
measured. Flush latency, scheduler delay, guard/CRC work and head publication
delay compete as causes of reduced service. Small WriteFile maxima and no output
fault argue against write-call blocking/failure as the sole measured bottleneck;
they do not measure total writer work or rule out combined causes. Producer
overhead cannot be isolated from arrivals recorded after hook work. WriteFile
timing excludes CRC work, copies, JSON, guards and disk checks; flush duration
includes blocking and scheduling rather than measuring device latency alone.

The replay projects the actual queue's serial capacity behavior onto **only
retained arrivals**, with instantaneous snapshot drains and no contention.
It is not actual compiled transport execution or recovered native arrival history.
Four phase choices at a 20-ms period yield 175, 0, 0 and 7 simulated refusals;
1-ms and 5-ms controls produce none. The 1,206.5674-ms periodic control is an
intentionally severe sensitivity case, not a claim that every real flush stalls.
Isolated pauses placed at startup and measured burst locations range from zero
to thousands of refusals; a 1,206.5674-ms pause at the 223-second bucket would
refuse 4,421 of 5,445 retained inputs from an empty queue. The unavailable lost
arrivals would change these numbers. The independent bin-count oracle agrees
with every phase and isolated-pause result. Competing schedules fit the retained
information; selecting one as B1 history would invent missing telemetry.

Prior compiled native controls are reauthenticated, not rerun or relabelled as
retail rates. D5X saturation intentionally pauses the writer during eight unpaced
producer streams: 718,988 capacity losses, 10,037 durable admissions and successful
post-reset recovery. Its timing stress also has an unpaced phase before paced
measurement. The 1,000 paced whole-fixture calls report hooked median/p95/p99/max
3.0/12.7/16.8/50.9 microseconds, versus baseline 0.2/0.2/0.3/2.1. Those include
fixture/scheduling effects; they cannot establish retail hot-path cost. The
eight-call concurrent control and STOP-under-production/crash/output-failure
controls establish their original bounded integrity behavior. They do not prove
loss-free service for B1's actual offered load.
